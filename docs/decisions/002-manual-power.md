# 002: Manual power, no motor

Date: 2026-09-25
Status: accepted

## Question

Is the crusher powered by the user's own strength or by a motor?

## Options considered

1. **Manual.** The user pushes a lever, or similar. Cheaper, quiet, nothing to
   plug in, simpler to assemble at home. Easier to make finger-safe, because
   the force stops the moment the user stops pushing.
2. **Motor.** It feels more like an appliance and takes no effort. But it
   costs more, is noisier, and needs electrical safety work: wiring, a power
   supply, sensors that stop the motor if a hand is inside. It is also harder
   to certify if the crusher is sold later.

## Decision

Manual. The exact form, such as a hand lever, a crank, or a foot pedal, is
chosen in Phase 3.

## Why

Manual fits the owner's other goals: cheap, a home-assembled kit, and safe for
hands. It also suits decision 001. The user's hands are already at the bottle
to screw the cap back on, and a manual mechanism can simply be held down while
they do it.

## What this means for the design

- The mechanism must multiply the user's force, for example with a long lever.
  How much depends on the Phase 2 bathroom-scale measurements.
- The design needs a way to hold the bottle crushed hands-free, such as a
  latch or a resting position, so both hands are free to put the cap back on.
